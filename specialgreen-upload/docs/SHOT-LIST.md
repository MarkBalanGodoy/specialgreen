# Photography Shot List

Twenty-four shots, ranked. Every one has a filename the site is already wired to, so a file
dropped into `assets/images/` with the right name appears on the site with no code change.

Whoever shoots this should read the two direction sections first. They decide whether the
site looks like an established regional company or a template.

---

## Direction: the crew

Latino landscaping and outdoor-service professionals, actively performing the work. This is
a deliberate casting choice and it reflects who does this work in South Louisiana.

They should read as experienced professionals:

- Clean, coordinated work clothing. Orange Special Green shirts, tucked, not fresh out of the
  bag. Work boots. Caps. Gloves where the job calls for them.
- Commercial-grade equipment, not homeowner gear. Zero-turn mowers, backpack blowers,
  commercial trimmers, a real work truck and trailer.
- **Actually working.** Hands on the machine, eyes on the job, mid-motion. Not standing in a
  line facing camera. Not arms folded by the truck. Not a thumbs up.

What to avoid: staged poses, forced smiles at the lens, anybody holding a tool they are
obviously not using, and the stock-photo tell of a spotless crew on a spotless lawn with no
evidence any work happened.

Grass clippings, a little dust, sweat on a Louisiana afternoon. That is what real looks like.

## Direction: the customer and the property

The properties do the positioning. Upscale Southern homes, established neighbourhoods,
large well-kept lawns, mature landscaping, professionally maintained beds, high-end
backyards and pools.

Where a customer appears, show a polished, successful homeowner talking naturally with a
crew leader on their own property. A real conversation, on site, both people engaged. Not a
handshake staged for the camera. Not pointing at nothing.

Do not cast customers by race. Communicate the market through the property, the styling and
the setting, which is what actually signals it.

## Direction: South Louisiana

The site should be unmistakably Baton Rouge and not Anywhere, USA.

- Green St. Augustine lawns, the wide-bladed grass, not northern fescue
- Mature live oaks with real canopy, Spanish moss where it occurs naturally
- Brick and stucco homes, established subdivisions
- Warm weather, humidity in the air, deep afternoon light
- Crepe myrtles, azaleas, palms and magnolias in the beds rather than northern plantings

## Technical

Send them **bright and untreated.** The site applies its own dark overlay, contrast and warm
grade. A bright, sharp source under a dark treatment reads cinematic; a dark source under a
dark treatment reads muddy. Do not pre-filter anything.

JPEG, sRGB, quality 85 or better, longest edge 2400px minimum. Shoot everything horizontal
**and** vertical where noted, because most of the traffic is on a phone.

## If Mark can only manage one afternoon

Shoot 1, 2, 5, 6 and 9 on one upscale property with the full crew working a normal job.
Those five carry most of the value, and the rest can be filled in over a season.

Get the before shot of every job from now on, even on a phone. It costs ten seconds and it
is the only image on this list that cannot be recreated later.
